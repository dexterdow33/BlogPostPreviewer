Working tools for New Hampshire’s Right-to-Know law. They cost nothing. A free email subscription opens them.

The text of the page is unchanged.

Filing a records request in New Hampshire takes no lawyer and no form. It does take a letter that cites the right paragraphs, a deadline counted correctly, and sometimes a clean way to black out a page before it goes anywhere. These tools do that work in your browser.

Opening them takes one email address and costs nothing. The same subscription sends one email when a new report publishes, and you can unsubscribe whenever you want. The reporting on this site stays open to everyone. The subscription opens the tools and the Record Room catalog.

What is inside

  • 91-A Request Builder. Writes a Right-to-Know request that cites RSA 91-A:4 and computes the five-business-day response date, skipping weekends and RSA 288:1 legal holidays.
  • Redact. Burns black boxes into a document as pixels, so the text underneath is destroyed rather than covered.
  • Granite State Record Room. The catalog of every document Granite State Report holds, with the source and rights holder named on each entry.
  • The Noncompliance List. Open to everyone with no subscription: the standing record of which public bodies meet their RSA 91-A duties and which do not. Read it here.
Nothing you type into these tools is sent to Granite State Report. The fields run in your browser. The page view itself is counted by the site’s analytics like any other page, as the Privacy Policy describes.
