The Noncompliance List

Independent New Hampshire Journalism · Northfield, NH
Right to Know · Standing Record

The Noncompliance List

Which New Hampshire public bodies meet their duties under the Right-to-Know Law, which do not, and the document behind every line.

Granite State Report · Version 1.0 · Current as of September 18, 2026

The office New Hampshire built to hear right-to-know complaints has had nobody in the chair for more than a year. Granite State Report has located no state agency that keeps a running count of how the law is obeyed, and no statute requires one to. So a reader who wants to know which town answered in five days and which took five months has to go and build that record by hand.

This is one attempt at it. The page has two halves, and the difference between them is the whole point. The first half holds findings by a tribunal: cases where the state’s own Right-to-Know Ombudsman decided that a public body broke the law. Granite State Report is not a party to any of them. The second half holds GSR’s own correspondence with public bodies, measured against four duties the statute spells out, with the document behind each line named. In that half GSR is the requester, and this page says so at the top of it rather than burying it at the bottom.

Bodies that meet the duty are listed as meeting it, and three of the six named below met every test applied to them. A record that only collects failures is a grievance, not a record.

7Bodies with a finding against them6Bodies in GSR’s documented record2Duties not met in that record

The four duties

Every body in the documented half is measured against the same four duties, in the same order. Three of them come straight out of one paragraph of the statute. The fourth is the body’s own word.

DutyStatuteWhat it requires
Five-day responseRSA 91-A:4, IV(b)Within 5 business days of a request, a public body must make the record available, deny the request, or provide a written statement of the time reasonably necessary to determine whether the request shall be granted or denied and the reason for the delay, with an itemized cost estimate if a charge would be incurred under paragraph VIII. The period is time to decide, not time to produce.
Written exemption statementRSA 91-A:4, IV(c)A body denying a request in whole or in part must provide a written statement of the specific exemption authorizing the withholding and a brief explanation of how the exemption applies to the record withheld.
FeesRSA 91-A:4, IV(d) and VIIINo cost or fee may be charged for inspection or delivery without copying, whether paper, electronic, or other form, except the per-electronic-communication charge paragraph VIII allows beyond 250 communications. Copies are at actual cost.
Kept its own dateRSA 91-A:4, IV(b)(3)Where a body answers with a written statement of the time reasonably necessary, that stated date is the body’s own commitment. GSR records whether it was met.
RSA 91-A:4, IV(b), as it is printed.
“(b) If a public body or agency is unable to make a governmental record available for immediate inspection and copying the public body or agency shall, within 5 business days of a request:
(1) Make such record available;
(2) Deny the request; or
(3) Provide a written statement of the time reasonably necessary to determine whether the request shall be granted or denied and the reason for the delay, and an itemized estimate of the cost of making the record available if a charge would be incurred under paragraph VIII.”
Read RSA 91-A:4 →

Read the numbering, because it carries weight. The itemized cost estimate belongs to option three alone. And the five days are time to decide, not time to produce: a body may answer inside the week with a written statement of the time reasonably necessary and the reason for the delay, and name a date months out. Whether the time it names is reasonably necessary is a judgment the statute leaves open and this page does not make. What this page records is whether the body then kept the date it set, which is its own word rather than the statute’s.

One point gets misstated constantly, including on printed forms handed to people at police counters. The access provision, RSA 91-A:4, I, runs to “Every citizen” and draws no distinction among them. There is no press tier. Paragraph IX of the same section does name a category of news organizations, and it does so for one purpose only, to carve them out of the public-interest fee waiver. It bears on cost, never on who may see a record.

Tier Two

Documented

Granite State Report is the requester in every matter below. No body in this tier has been found by any tribunal to have violated RSA 91-A on these facts. Each entry states what the documents show and nothing further.

Granite State Report’s own record of correspondence with each body. GSR is the requester in every matter in this tier. Nothing here is a legal finding. Each line rests on a dated document, and the four tests are applied the same way to every body.

Across the bodies named below, 9 of the duties tested were met, 2 were not, and 2 remain open. Two things are worth saying about that before anyone reads it as a finding about New Hampshire. It is four bodies with a measurable clock, all of them correspondents GSR chose to write to. And it points the opposite way from Tier One above, where six of the seven adjudicated violations are failures of that same five-day duty.

The honest version of the observation is narrow and still interesting. In GSR’s own file, every body whose clock could be measured answered inside five days. Both failures came afterward and both are exemption statements: one department withheld records naming no exemption at all, and another refused five items on a single statutory citation without saying how it applied to any of them. The duty agencies fail badly enough to end up before a tribunal is the same one they meet when a reporter is holding a stopwatch. That is a pattern worth watching. It is not yet a finding, and this page will not call it one until the record is wide enough to carry it.

Held. Three further matters are on file and are not named here. Under GSR’s standing rule a body is named only after the reporting that concerns it has published, or after the body has received the entry and a request for comment with a stated deadline. Those three are not included in any total on this page, and will be added when that condition is met.

What is kept off this list

A list is defined as much by what it refuses to carry.

  • Any matter in which Dexter Dow is a requester in his personal capacity rather than on behalf of Granite State Report, or is a named subject of the records sought. Those are his own records fights and they do not belong on a list about public accountability.
  • Requests made under statutes other than RSA 91-A, including case-record requests under RSA 170-G:8-a, which carry different duties and different clocks.
  • Any body about which GSR holds a single data point. One interaction is never an entry.
  • Anything GSR cannot show a reader in a document.

How a body gets on the list, and how it gets off

Documents decide. Every line rests on a dated document a reader can be shown. Where a date is not in the record, this page says so instead of estimating it.

The same four tests for everyone. Every body is measured against the same four duties, in the same order, whether GSR likes the answer or not. Bodies that pass are listed passing.

Business days are computed, not judged. The five-business-day clock runs from the day after the body receives the request, excluding Saturdays, Sundays, and New Hampshire legal holidays. The computation is shown.

Right of reply before naming. Before a body is named in Tier 2, it receives the entry and the documents behind it, with ten business days to respond. Its response is published in full. If none arrives the page says no response was received by the date. It never says the body refused.

GSR does not call it a violation. Tier 2 says what a document shows and which duty it bears on. Only a tribunal decides that a body broke the law, and those decisions live in Tier 1 under the tribunal’s name.

Corrections stay. A correction carries what the page said, what is true, what changed, and the date and time of the fix. It runs at the top of the entry, it stays permanently, and it goes out on the channels that carried the original.

Entries come off. A Tier 2 entry closes when the body produces the records or supplies what the duty required. The closed entry stays visible with its resolution date, because a record that disappears when it is satisfied is not a record.

How the clock is computed. The five-business-day period runs from the day after the public body receives the request. Saturdays, Sundays, and the legal holidays New Hampshire state offices observe are excluded. No holiday falls inside any window computed on this page. Where a request date is not in GSR’s record, no five-day result is stated for that body.
